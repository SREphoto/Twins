# Glove Box (Inert Atmosphere Glove Box)

## Operation Principle

A glove box (also called an inert atmosphere box or dry box) is a sealed enclosure that allows manipulation of materials in a controlled, inert atmosphere (typically argon or nitrogen). The operator accesses the interior through gloves (butyl, neoprene, or hypalon) that are sealed to ports in the front wall. Materials are transferred in and out through an antechamber (transfer chamber/airlock) that can be evacuated and purged with inert gas to prevent contamination of the main chamber atmosphere. A purification system removes oxygen and moisture from the recirculating atmosphere, typically maintaining levels below 1 ppm O₂ and 1 ppm H₂O. The glove box is essential for air- and moisture-sensitive chemistry, including handling of organometallics, Grignard reactions, lithium chemistry, and OLED/photovoltaic materials.

## Key Functions

1. **Air-sensitive synthesis** — handle reactive organometallic compounds
2. **Moisture-free environment** — work with hygroscopic materials
3. **Inert atmosphere storage** — store air-sensitive chemicals/samples
4. **OLED/photovoltaic fabrication** — device assembly in controlled atmosphere
5. **Battery research** — assemble lithium-ion/next-gen batteries
6. **Pharmaceutical development** — handle potent compounds (with HEPA/charcoal filtration)

## Physical Specifications

### Standard 2-Glove Configuration (Single Workstation)

- **Chamber Dimensions (W×D×H)**: 36" × 24" × 24" (914 × 610 × 610 mm)
- **Overall Dimensions (with antechamber)**: ~48" × 28" × 60" (1219 × 711 × 1524 mm)
- **Chamber Material**: Acrylic (transparent) or polycarbonate — 0.5–1.0" (12.7–25.4 mm) thick
- **Back Panel**: Opaque (welded aluminum, stainless steel, or epoxy-coated steel)
- **Front/Sides**: Clear acrylic for visibility
- **Glove Port Diameter**: 8" (203 mm) — two ports on 2-glove box
- **Glove Port Spacing**: ~18" (457 mm) center-to-center
- **Antechamber (Transfer Chamber)**:
  - Diameter: ~10" (254 mm)
  - Length: ~14" (356 mm)
  - Inner Door: Manual or automatic, seals against O-ring
  - Outer Door: Manual or automatic
  - Purge/Vacuum Port: 1/4" Swagelok fitting
- **Pressure Gauge**: ±15" H₂O (0–30 mbar) — manometer on antechamber and main chamber
- **HEPA Filter**: On gas inlet/outlet (0.3 µm)
- **Weight**: ~300–500 lb (136–227 kg)
- **Power**: 120 VAC, 15–20 A

### Standard 4-Glove Configuration (Dual Workstation)

- **Chamber Dimensions (W×D×H)**: 72" × 24" × 30" (1829 × 610 × 762 mm)
- **Overall Dimensions**: ~84" × 30" × 64" (2134 × 762 × 1626 mm)
- **Glove Ports**: 4 (two per operator position)
- **Antechamber**: Larger — 14" diameter × 18" length (or dual antechambers)
- **Weight**: ~600–1000 lb (272–454 kg)
- **Power**: 120–240 VAC, 20–30 A

### Glove Ports

- **Port Ring Material**: Anodized aluminum, powder-coated steel, or stainless steel
- **Port Diameter**: 6" (152 mm) or 8" (203 mm) opening
- **Port Depth**: ~3–4" (76–102 mm)
- **Glove Retention**: O-ring groove + snap ring or clamp ring
- **Port Spacing**: ~18" (457 mm) center-to-center for single-operator
- **Port Height from Work Surface**: ~6–8" (152–203 mm)

### Gloves

- **Materials**:
  - **Butyl Rubber (IIR)**: Best barrier for moisture and oxygen; good resistance to most solvents; standard for inert atmosphere glove boxes
  - **Neoprene (CR)**: Good general chemical resistance; lower cost; moderate barrier
  - **Hypalon (CSM)**: Excellent ozone resistance; good for strong acids
  - **Viton® (FKM)**: Excellent solvent resistance; poor for moisture barrier
  - **Latex/Nitrile**: For biological/cleanroom use — not suitable for inert atmosphere
- **Sizes**: 7 to 10 (hand length); 8" and 14" port depth sleeves
- **Gauge**: 0.015"–0.030" (0.38–0.76 mm) thickness
- **Sleeve Length**:
  - 8" port (standard): ~24" (610 mm) total reach
  - 14" port (deep): ~32" (813 mm) total reach
- **Glove Box Connection**: O-ring seal between sleeve and port ring
- **Cuff**: Rolled cuff at glove end or integrated O-ring cuff
- **Markings**: Size printed on wrist, material type

### Antechamber (Transfer Chamber / Airlock)

- **Purpose**: Transfer materials between the external environment and the main chamber without contaminating the inert atmosphere
- **Construction**: 304 stainless steel or clear acrylic tube
- **Inner Door**: Opens into main chamber — seals with O-ring (silicone or Viton)
- **Outer Door**: Opens to outside — seals with O-ring
- **Doors**: Hinged or sliding; manual latch or automatic pneumatic
- **Purge Sequence**: Evacuate (vacuum) → Refill (inert gas) → Repeat 3–5 cycles
- **Vacuum Connection**: 1/4" or 3/8" Swagelok or KF-16 flange
- **Pressure Gauge**: Built-in aneroid or digital manometer, ±15" H₂O
- **Shelving**: Shelves inside antechamber for organizing transfers

### Pressure Gauge

- **Type**: Manometer (differential pressure)
- **Range**: ±15" H₂O (±30 mbar) or ±20" H₂O
- **Display**: Analog dial or digital LED
- **Unit**: inches H₂O (inH₂O), mbar, hPa
- **Location**: Visible on face of glove box or on antechamber
- **Function**: Indicates positive/negative pressure differential between chamber and ambient

### Purification System

- **Type**: Catalytic purification (removes O₂ and H₂O)
- **Oxygen Removal**: Copper catalyst (reduced CuO → Cu → CuO + O₂ → CuO)
- **Moisture Removal**: Molecular sieve (4Å or 13X zeolite)
- **Regeneration**: Heated (200–300°C) purge with 5% H₂/N₂ mixture
- **Typical Purity**: < 1 ppm O₂, < 1 ppm H₂O
- **Gas Consumption**: ~0.5–2 ft³ per purge cycle
- **Circulation Fan**: Blower pushes chamber atmosphere through purification columns at ~30–100 CFM
- **Oxygen Sensor**: Electrochemical or paramagnetic cell
- **Moisture Sensor**: Capacitive or aluminum oxide sensor

### UV Light (Optional)

- **Type**: UV-C (254 nm) germicidal lamp
- **Wavelength**: 254 nm
- **Power**: 15–30 W
- **Location**: Mounted inside chamber ceiling
- **Use**: Sterilization for biological/cleanroom applications
- **Safety**: Interlock — UV shuts off when main door opens
- **Timer**: 15–60 minute setting

### Internal Electrical Outlets

- **Type**: GFCI-protected, 120 VAC (or 240 VAC)
- **Quantity**: 2–4 outlets
- **Location**: Mounted on back wall or work surface
- **Pass-through**: Feed-through ports for wiring, tubing, sensors (typically 1" diameter)
- **Power Entry**: Sealed power entry panel at back of chamber

### Internal Work Surfaces

- **Material**: Stainless steel (304, 18-gauge) or epoxy resin
- **Height**: ~6" (152 mm) above chamber floor
- **Dimensions**: Full width × 18–22" (457–559 mm) depth
- **Load Capacity**: ~50–100 lb distributed
- **Features**: Removable for cleaning; raised edge to contain spills

## Usage Procedure

### Startup

1. **Connect services**: Inert gas (argon or nitrogen, 99.999% purity), vacuum pump, electrical power
2. **Set gas pressure**: Regulator set to 15–20 psi supply to glove box
3. **Open main gas valve**: Supply inert gas to purification system
4. **Turn on purification system**: Circulate chamber atmosphere
5. **Monitor O₂/H₂O sensors**: Allow to stabilize (< 1 ppm target)
6. **Set pressure controller**: Maintain ~0.05–0.15" H₂O positive pressure
7. **Check gloves**: Inspect for pinholes, tears, or degradation

### Transferring Materials In (Antechamber Purge Cycle)

1. **Outer door closed, inner door closed** (both sealed)
2. **Open outer door** (vent antechamber to atmosphere first if needed)
3. **Place materials in antechamber**:
   - Open containers outside; seal inside to prevent spillage if vacuum is applied
   - Glassware/equipment should be baked or vacuum-dried before transfer
4. **Close outer door** — ensure O-ring seal
5. **Purge cycle options**:
   - **Vacuum/purge**: Evacuate antechamber (to 100–500 mTorr) → Refill with inert gas → Repeat 3–5×
   - **Flow purge**: Continuous inert gas flow through antechamber for 10–30 minutes
6. **Open inner door**: Pull materials into main chamber
7. **Close inner door**

### Working Inside

1. **Insert hands into gloves**: Slide arms fully into sleeves
2. **Check glove mobility**: Rotate wrists, flex fingers
3. **Handle materials**: Use normal techniques — all manipulation done through gloves
4. **Use internal equipment**: Stirrers, balances, heaters, chillers
5. **Monitor O₂/H₂O**: Keep below 1 ppm — if levels rise, initiate purge cycle
6. **Replace tools/samples**: Use antechamber for transfers
7. **Hot work**: Place hot objects on cooling plates before sealing in containers
8. **Solvent use**: Solvents can saturate purification catalyst — minimize exposure; use charcoal filter

### Shutdown

1. **Seal all samples/chemicals**: In gas-tight containers before removal
2. **Transfer materials out**: Reverse of transfer in (use antechamber)
3. **Turn off purification**: Cycle down per manufacturer instructions
4. **Close main gas valve**: Shut off inert gas supply
5. **Turn off vacuum pump**: Close valve between pump and glove box
6. **Turn off power**: Main power switch

### Maintenance

- **Weekly**: Check glove integrity (visual); check O₂/H₂O levels; check pressure gauge zero
- **Monthly**: Regenerate purification columns (per manufacturer cycle); clean antechamber O-rings; verify vacuum pump oil level
- **Quarterly**: Replace HEPA filters; lubricate door hinges/latches; check glove port O-rings
- **Annually**: Replace gloves; perform leak test (pressure decay test); calibrate O₂/H₂O sensors; replace vacuum pump oil
- **As needed**:
  - Regenerate columns when O₂/H₂O rise above 1 ppm
  - Replace charcoal filter if solvent odor is noticeable
  - Replace desiccant in vent filters

## Safety Considerations

- **Oxygen depletion hazard**: Inert gas (N₂, Ar) displaces oxygen — breathing atmosphere inside glove box is lethal
- **Positive pressure required**: Always maintain positive pressure — negative pressure can collapse the chamber and/or allow air ingress
- **Glove failure**: If a glove tears, immediately flush chamber with inert gas and leave the area (O₂ alarm will sound)
- **Glove port door**: Install port plugs when gloves are not in use
- **Overpressure**: Never exceed rated pressure (±15" H₂O) — chamber walls can crack or blow out
- **Chemical exposure**: Glove material can be permeated by solvents — change gloves regularly; use proper gloves for solvent
- **UV exposure**: Never operate UV light with gloves off or with chamber open — eye/skin damage
- **Heavy weight**: Glove boxes are heavy — place on reinforced bench or floor stand
- **Fire hazard**: Some internal equipment (hotplates, stirrers) can ignite flammable solvents in the chamber
- **Gas cylinder storage**: Keep inert gas cylinders secured, away from exits

| ------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------- |
| Atmosphere          | Inert (Ar/N₂)           | Room air            |
| Operator protection | Barrier (gloves)        | Airflow barrier     |
| Environment         | Sealed, controlled      | Open, ventilated    |
| O₂/H₂O Level        | < 1 ppm                 | Ambient             |
| Use                 | Air-sensitive chemistry | Hazardous chemistry |
