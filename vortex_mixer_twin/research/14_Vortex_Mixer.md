# Vortex Mixer

## Operation Principle

A vortex mixer uses a motor-driven eccentric mechanism to create circular (orbital) vibration in a cup head, transmitting energy to a tube. The eccentric weight on the motor shaft creates powerful vibration. When a tube is pressed into the rubber cup head, vibration is transferred to the liquid, creating a rapidly rotating vortex. Touch-start models run only when downward pressure is applied; continuous models run unattended. Speeds: 200–3200 rpm.

## Key Functions

1. **Sample mixing** — homogenizing liquid reagents
2. **Resuspension** — re-dissolving pellets after centrifugation
3. **Cell disruption** — with beads for small volumes
4. **Enzyme reaction mixing** — quick component mixing
5. **Solvent extraction** — micro-extractions

## Physical Features on the 3D Model

### Base Unit

- **Housing** — cast metal or ABS, ~100–200 × 100–150 mm
- **Internal Ballast** — weight for stable operation
- **Suction Feet** — 4 rubber suction cups, 20–30 mm
- **Ventilation** — grille on bottom/sides

### Motor System

- **Drive Motor** — universal or induction, 10–60 W
- **Eccentric Cam** — off-center weight on motor shaft
- **Ball Bearings** — for smooth rotation
- **Spring Mechanism** — for touch mode

### Cup Head

- **Rubber Cup** — concave, ~20–30 mm diameter, 10–15 mm deep
- **Material** — natural rubber or silicone
- **Replaceable** — push-fit on drive spindle

### Attachments

- **Microtiter Plate Platform** — flat for 96-well plates
- **Tube Platform** — holds 5–15 tubes simultaneously
- **Locking Mechanism** — spring-loaded clips
